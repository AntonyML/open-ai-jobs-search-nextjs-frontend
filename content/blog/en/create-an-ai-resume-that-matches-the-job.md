A strong resume is not a generic autobiography. It is a clear explanation of why your real experience is relevant to a specific role.

## Start with one reliable base resume

Create a complete version with your work history, education, skills, achievements, and contact details. This version should be factual and reusable. Do not let an AI tool add experience, tools, titles, or results that you cannot support.

## Read the job description as a requirements document

Separate the posting into responsibilities, required skills, preferred skills, seniority, and outcomes. Then compare those requirements with your real experience. The goal is relevance, not keyword stuffing.

## Tailor the evidence, not the truth

Adapt the order of sections, summary, skills, and achievement bullets to emphasize the strongest evidence for that role. Keep dates, employers, titles, and measurable results accurate.

## Check ATS readability

Use conventional headings, selectable text, readable dates, and a simple layout. A visually impressive document that an applicant tracking system cannot parse is not an effective application.

## Keep a version for every application

Saving tailored versions helps you remember what you submitted and makes interview preparation easier. CVMeld is designed around this workflow: one source profile, multiple job-specific documents.
